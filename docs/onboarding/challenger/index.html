Your first system-design interview

Twenty minutes, one design, and an assessor who has read it

You will be given a vague product brief by an interviewer who answers only what you ask, write a design document in the browser, submit it, and then defend your own decisions. This guide walks the whole session, app by app.

The shape of a session

Timebox
about 20 minutes from brief to submitted design
People
an interviewer (facts only) and an assessor (questions only)
You produce
DESIGN.md, optionally TICKETS.md
Graded on
discovery, scoping, stakeholders, communication
Extra credit
implementation tickets an engineer could build from

    01

    Sign in with the email your instructor gave you

    Where: the /login page, from the link in your welcome email

    Your instructor or admin creates your account. You receive a set-password email from the simulator; follow its link, choose a password, and you land back on the sign-in page. Sign in with that email and password.

    • No email arrived? Check spam, then ask your instructor to resend it. They can do that from the admin dashboard.
    • Forgot your password? Use Forgot password? on the sign-in page. A reset link goes to the same email.
    • See "Create challenger account"? Only use it if your instructor told you to self-register. Otherwise sign in with the account they made, so your sessions are assigned to the right person.
    Where you landAfter signing in you see Your sessions: the challenges assigned to you. Nothing there yet means your instructor has not created a session for you. There is nothing for you to set up on your own machine for an interview scenario.
    02

    Settings: your name and, optionally, your own keys

    Where: Settings on your dashboard

    Three cards. Only the first is required.

    CardWhat it doesDo you need it?
    Display nameHow instructors see you in the directory and in replays.Yes. Set it once.
    Groq API keyThe personas and the grader run on a language model. The classroom has a shared key; if you add your own, your sessions use yours instead, so a busy class never slows you down.Optional. Recommended if your instructor asks, or if replies feel slow.
    GitHub tokenOnly used by build scenarios that read a repo you pushed. Interview scenarios never touch GitHub.Not for interviews. Skip it unless you are later assigned a build scenario.

    Getting a Groq key

    1. Open console.groq.com/keys and sign in (free account).
    2. Press Create API Key, give it a name such as flight-sim, and copy the key. It starts with gsk_ and is shown once.
    3. Back in Settings, paste it into Groq API key and press Save key. The simulator checks it against Groq immediately, then stores it encrypted. It is never displayed again; Remove key deletes it.

    Getting a GitHub token (build scenarios only)

    1. Open github.com/settings/tokens. GitHub's own walkthrough is at Managing your personal access tokens.
    2. Create a token. No scopes or permissions are needed: the simulator only reads public repos, the token just raises your API rate limit. A 90-day expiry is fine.
    3. Paste it into GitHub token and press Save token. Same treatment as the Groq key: checked, encrypted, never shown again.
    Why keys are per personLanguage-model and GitHub calls are rate-limited per key. When everyone in a class shares one key, the slowest moment is the whole class submitting at once. Your own key gives your sessions their own lane.
    03

    Open your session and meet the workstation

    Where: Your sessions → Open

    Each assigned session has an Open button. It takes you to the workstation: a desktop with a dock of apps along the bottom. The desktop's subtitle tells you which kind of scenario you are in. For an interview it reads: ask the interviewer clarifying questions, write DESIGN.md in Files, Submit it, then defend your decisions with the assessor.

    DockTeam ChatThe interview happens here. A people rail on the left: the interviewer first, the assessor appears later.
    DockFilesYour workspace with a code editor. DESIGN.md and TICKETS.md are already there, plus a brief in docs/.
    DockSubmitOne button: Submit DESIGN.md. It sends the file exactly as it is in Files.
    DockTicketsA board with one seeded ticket: the extra-credit ask to write implementation tickets.
    DockMailThreaded email with the cast. Interview scenarios rarely use it; build scenarios do.
    Not shownWorkspaceHidden for interviews. Your folder is created automatically the first time you open Files.
    If the page says "This session is not assigned to you"You opened a link made for someone else, or your instructor has not assigned it yet. Go back to your dashboard and open a session from the list, or ask for a fresh link.
    04

    Read the brief in Team Chat, and notice how little it says

    Where: Team Chat, the interviewer's conversation

    The moment the session starts, the interviewer posts the problem. It is deliberately short. Here is the one from the parking-garage interview:

    SolA garage wants a display and an API: how many spaces are free, and which floor. Design the system. Twenty minutes.
    Session started. Online: Sol, Rowan.

    Everything you need to design well is missing on purpose: how big, how fresh, what happens when sensors fail, who reads the numbers. The interviewer has those facts and will hand them over only when you ask the right question. That is the first thing you are graded on.

    05

    Ask clarifying questions until the constraints are on the table

    Where: Team Chat, about the first 5 minutes

    The interviewer answers with facts and never with architecture. Ask about scale, freshness, failure, and who consumes the output. Each good question unlocks a little more; the chat shows a signal line when that happens.

    YouHow many spaces are we talking about, and how is occupancy sensed?
    SolA few hundred spaces over a handful of floors. Loop sensors on the ramps. They are not perfect.
    [reveal] Sol opened up (rung 1).
    YouHow fresh does the lobby display need to be?
    SolHalf a minute behind is fine. People are not racing F1.

    Questions that usually pay

    • Scale. How many users, items, events per second? Peak versus average?
    • Freshness and consistency. How stale can a read be? Does anyone need exactly-once?
    • Failure. What breaks in the real world (sensors lie, networks drop, clients retry)?
    • Consumers and reports. Who reads this, on what schedule, and what do they do with it?
    • Non-goals. What is explicitly out of v1?
    What does not workAsking "what architecture do you want?" or "should I use a queue?" The interviewer will say it is your call. Asking nothing and designing from the brief alone leaves the discovery score near zero, however elegant the design.
    06

    Write DESIGN.md in the Files editor

    Where: Files. Save with Ctrl+S (⌘+S on a Mac); it also autosaves

    Open Files. DESIGN.md opens automatically in an editor with syntax highlighting. It already carries the section headings the assessor and grader expect; fill them in, in your own words, from the facts you extracted.

    SectionWhat goes there
    1. ProblemThe brief restated in your words, with the constraints you uncovered.
    2. Clarifying questionsWhat you asked and the answers you are designing against.
    3. RequirementsFunctional, non-functional (scale, latency, consistency, availability), and non-goals for v1.
    4. Capacity sketchRough numbers: QPS, storage, payload size. Show the arithmetic.
    5. APIEndpoints or messages, with shapes.
    6. High-level architectureComponents and how a request flows. The simulator draws a diagram from this section.
    7. Data modelTables, keys, indexes, retention.
    8. Key decisions and trade-offsEach decision, the alternative you rejected, and why. This is what the assessor will probe.
    • The Preview button renders the markdown so you can read it as the assessor will.
    • A gold dot on the tab means unsaved changes. Save writes to your workspace; the server also keeps a copy, so a refresh or a dropped connection does not lose your work.
    • The status line at the bottom confirms each save and shows the workspace revision.
    Design for the facts you extractedIn the garage example, the sensors double-count and drop events. A design that treats them as a perfect counter scores low on stakeholders however clean it looks. Reconciliation paths, overrides, and staleness budgets are where the marks are.
    07

    Optional: write implementation tickets for extra credit

    Where: TICKETS.md in Files, or the Tickets board

    The Tickets board is seeded with one ask: write tickets so an engineer who has only the tickets, not DESIGN.md, could implement this system. If you have time after the design, do it in TICKETS.md or as tickets on the board. Each ticket needs context, the interface or data shape, and acceptance criteria.

    Extra credit is scored separately and can lift your total by up to 15 percentage points. It never lowers it. Whoever grades chooses whether to include it with the tickets extra credit toggle.

    08

    Submit DESIGN.md

    Where: Submit → Submit DESIGN.md

    The Submit app has one button for interview scenarios. It reads DESIGN.md (and TICKETS.md if you wrote one) from your workspace as they are right now, records the submission, and draws a diagram of your architecture. Save first if the tab still shows a gold dot.

    • You can submit again after edits; the latest submission is the one the assessor and grader read.
    • "DESIGN.md is empty" means the file has no content yet. Write it in Files first.
    • Telling the interviewer "I'm done" in chat is fine, but it does not submit. Only the Submit button does.

    The moment the submission lands, the assessor joins Team Chat.

    09

    Defend your decisions with the assessor

    Where: Team Chat, the assessor's conversation (unread badge)
    [reveal] Rowan is here to assess your design.
    RowanIn section 8 you chose per-floor counters in Redis and reconcile against the ramp sensors every minute. Walk me through what happens when a sensor double-counts for an hour.

    The assessor has read your document and asks one probing question at a time about something you wrote. They do not propose a better design, teach the textbook answer, or reveal constraints you never asked about. They are checking two things: that you made the decision, and that you can defend it.

    • Answer from your document. "In section 4 I estimated …" is a strong opening.
    • A defensible alternative is fine. "I chose X over Y because Z" beats guessing what they want.
    • If you realise something is wrong, say so and say what you would change. That is graded as communication, not as failure.
    • Vague answers get "point me at where your document covers that." Have it open in Files while you talk.
    Before you submit, the assessor stays silentMessaging them early gets a short reply that they join after you submit. That is expected.
    10

    Grade the run and read the feedback

    Where: Team Chat → Grade run (top right)

    Grading reads the whole transcript (your questions and the defense), your submitted design, the diagram, and the tickets if the toggle is on. It scores four criteria and cites evidence for each.

    CriterionWeightWhat it measures
    Discovery2.0Did your questions extract the real constraints without being fed the answer?
    Scoping2.0Detail, correctness, and whether a staff engineer could understand the document.
    Stakeholders1.0Did you treat it as a real system: API, data, scale, consistency, failure, a v1 cut?
    Communication2.0Do the assessment answers show you wrote and can defend this design?
    TicketsextraCould an engineer implement from the tickets alone? Up to +15 points on top.

    The engineer level your instructor set (intern through distinguished) changes how demanding the personas are and how the criteria are weighted. A senior-level session expects a v1 cut and explicit trade-offs; an intern-level one rewards asking and clear thinking.

    Read the caveatUntil the grader has been calibrated against instructor scores, the grade is marked directional. Your instructor's review of the replay is the grade that counts.
    11

    When something looks wrong

    Quick fixes before you ask for help
    Team Chat shows "Disconnected" or replies never arrive

    Refresh the page. The chat reconnects and replays the transcript. If replies are slow but arriving, the classroom key is busy; adding your own Groq key in Settings gives your session its own lane.

    The assessor will not talk to me

    They join only after a submission. Open Submit and press Submit DESIGN.md; if it reports the file is empty, write it in Files first.

    Save failed in Files

    Your text is still in the editor. Check the status line for the reason (a very large file is refused, and paths inside .git are managed for you), then save again. The tab keeps its gold dot until a save succeeds.

    Files says "No workspace yet"

    That message belongs to build scenarios. For an interview the workspace appears on its own; if it does not, refresh and open Files again. If it still fails, the server could not create your folder and your instructor needs to look at it.

    "Your sign-in has expired"

    Sign in again from /login; you return to the same session. Nothing you saved is lost.
